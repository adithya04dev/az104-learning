Azure RBAC · Effective access

Turn “needs access” into a precise assignment.

Pulse needs engineers, auditors, and an automated release service. Your job is to translate each requirement into who, what actions, and where—then predict inherited access.

≈20 minutesAZ-104: Manage accessLeast privilege

One role assignment has three coordinates

Principal · Who?User, group, service principal, or managed identity.
Role · What?A role definition that bundles allowed operations.
Scope · Where?Management group, subscription, resource group, or resource.

Exam algorithm: identify the principal → list the required actions → choose the narrowest built-in role → choose the narrowest workable scope → verify prerequisites and inherited assignments.

Authentication, directory administration, and resource authorization are separate

Microsoft Entra role

Delegates administration of directory objects or Entra capabilities—for example, managing users or authentication policy.

Azure RBAC role

Authorizes management-plane or data-plane actions against Azure resources at a Resource Manager scope.

Classic distractor

The User Administrator cannot restart a VM

User Administrator is a Microsoft Entra role. It does not grant VM management. Conversely, Virtual Machine Contributor does not grant authority to create users in the directory.

Scope is a hierarchy, and children inherit

Tenant root management group
└── Product management group
   └── Pulse subscription ← Reader assigned here
      └── rg-pulse-prod ← inherited Reader
         └── api-vm-01 ← inherited Reader

A role assigned at a parent scope is inherited by children. Assigning at a lower scope does not travel upward or sideways. Effective access can be the combination of multiple assignments from the user and their group memberships, subject to controls such as deny assignments.

Narrowest scope does not mean “always individual resource.” Choose the narrowest scope that covers the complete operational responsibility without multiplying assignments needlessly.

Know the broad built-ins—and distrust their convenience

RoleCore capabilityImportant boundary
ReaderView control-plane resourcesDoes not change them; data access may require a data role.
ContributorManage resourcesCannot assign Azure RBAC roles.
OwnerManage resources and accessHighly privileged; avoid as a default.
Role Based Access Control AdministratorManage Azure RBAC accessDoes not automatically grant general resource management.

Service-specific roles are often safer. A person who only reads blobs needs a data-plane role such as Storage Blob Data Reader, not broad Contributor.

Worked product decisions

Pulse production support

Restart VMs, but do not manage access

Start from a VM-specific built-in role and scope it to the production resource group if the team manages every VM there. Contributor is broader than the described requirement; Owner is much broader.

Release automation

An app deploys only to one web application

Use a service principal or managed identity as the principal, select the smallest suitable deployment role, and scope it to that app where practical. Do not use a shared human account.

External audit

An auditor must inspect resources in one subscription

Invite or represent the auditor appropriately, then assign Reader at the subscription. That single scope intentionally covers all child resource groups and resources.

Check your decisions

1. A team must manage all VMs in one resource group but must not assign access. Which approach is best?

2. Reader is assigned to a group at subscription scope. A member of that group opens a child resource group. What should you expect?
